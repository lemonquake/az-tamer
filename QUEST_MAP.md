| 15 years ago | **The Legion War.** Nine corrupted four-element Guardians mass their armies in Ghandra. Three best friends — **Aljay the Dawnflame, Greggy the Stormheart, Onnel the Worldroot** — walk in with nine bonded lights and seal the Legion away. The seal thins a little every year. |
| Now | You graduate. The engine in the Stormspire starts answering something inside the seal. And then the legends stop answering their radios… |

### The four continents (and the places you'll actually walk)
- **Olivar** — the Capital Region. **Haven City** (the five Grand Houses, shops, Sanctum, Coliseum, Expedition Gate) and the **Tamer University of Aurel**. In the far north of Olivar stands **Hyujon, the Lantern of the North** — a high-tech city built on tamed old-empire engine-craft *(Act II)*.
- **Veyra** — the western sea-continent of a thousand harbors. The **Pearlwake Compact** guild fleet.
- **Tharkand** — the eastern red waste. Dead war-engines, the **Duneward Assembly**.
- **Noruun** — the frozen south. The **Aurora Sentinel Lodge** watches Ghandra's reflection in the auroras.
- **Agdao Island** — the Cradle of Tamers, in the western sea. Hut villages, the lagoon, the **Circle of the First Fire** (the oldest guild in the world), and lately: one retired thunderhead named Greggy.
- **Ghandra** — the dimension folded into the center of the world. On no map. Behind one seal.

## PART III — THE SURVIVAL CODEX *(read this if Mossdeep keeps killing you)*

Mossdeep is not a wall — it's a syllabus. The game's combat runs on four economies, and
the dungeon punishes ignoring any of them:

1. **The SP economy.** Techniques are not free. **👊 Strike builds SP** (+~8% per hit) and
   **🛡️ Guard restores SP** (+12%) while halving damage. The rhythm is *Strike/Guard to
   charge, Technique to cash in.* If you spam techniques on round one you will be dry and
   dying by round three. Open every fight with a Strike.
2. **The element economy.** Damage is multiplicative across every defender element
   (clamped ×0.4–×2.5). Fire roasts Nature/Ice; Water drowns Fire; Nature eats Water;
   Electric shocks Water; Rock blunts almost everything physical. A "Devastating!" hit does
   roughly **triple** a "…resisted." one — target selection IS the difficulty slider.
   Mossdeep is full of Nature/Rock (Pebblit) and Water (Mistling): bring Fire for the foxes
   and shrooms, Nature for the puddles, and never throw Electric at a Pebblit.
3. **The body economy.** One Guardian cannot tank 3v1. **Fill your party to 3 before
   floor 2** — gift Sweet Berries (◆30 at Pina's) to wild Guardians, win, and bonded ones
   ask to join. Novice wilds join *easily* (base willingness ~50–55%). Two berries into a
   Pebblit is a tank for the whole act.
4. **The Crawler economy.** A **Bore Cannon (◆700)** gives a 10% chance the whole enemy
   line *skips round one*. Better Legs make steps free; better Scanner shows the stairs.
   Heal at the **Sanctum (south Haven City, free)** between runs — there is no shame in it,
   only EXP retention.

> **A note on Rank.** Your Universal Rank runs on **Tournament Points and nothing else** —
> battles, captures, quests and dungeon clears build your career, but the ladder only moves
> when you place in sanctioned tournaments (the Grand Coliseum's brackets, the Turmal
> Seasonal, the intercontinental circuits). Everyone starts a Beginner. Champions are *made*
> in the Ring.

> **The five-minute Mossdeep plan:** Sanctum heal → buy 4 berries + 2 tonics → floor 1:
> befriend a Pebblit and a Mistling → you now outnumber problems → Strike to charge,
> Devastating techniques only → clear. Chapter I is half done and you're Lv 7+.

## PART IV — ACT II: **THE ANOMALIES** *(the next arc — canonical design bible)*

> *Fifteen years ago, nine generals were sealed behind one door.
> Somebody has started praying at it.*

### The inciting wound — the Silence of Legends

It begins with static. **Greggy the Stormheart cannot raise Onnel or Aljay.** Onnel never
much liked the communicator — they answer one call in five, always have, the Worldroot
keeps slow time. **But Aljay always replies.** Late, rambling, mid-laugh — but *always*.
Eleven weeks of nothing. And when Greggy pulled the relay logs to triangulate them, he
found something worse than silence: **their traces are gone.** Not old. Not faded.
*Wiped.* No relay history, no guild-ledger entries, no sighting reports — nine years of
Veyl-grade paper trail, erased like a chalk line in rain. Silence is a choice. Erasure is
a *job*, done by someone with reach.

And reach is exactly what the new horror on everyone's lips has.

### The enemy — THE ANOMALIES

A terror-cult that worships the **nine corrupted Guardians of Ghandra as imprisoned
gods**. They call the Legion War "the Great Theft" and the seal "the Ninefold Insult."
They do not recruit; they *convert*, the way rot converts a forest. They surfaced quietly
— defaced seal-shrines, stolen Legion-war relics, missing wardens — until the night they
stopped being quiet: **the invasion of Hyujon**, the high-tech Lantern of the North,
chosen, the broadsheets assumed, for its salvaged war-engine grid. Greggy doesn't buy it.
The Anomalies don't improvise, and they don't escalate without *cause*. His private fear,
the one he'll only say out loud on his own bluff with the wind covering his voice:
**someone gave them a reason. And the two people most likely to have kicked that
particular hive are the two people no one can find.**

The cult moves as **four Factions**, each elite, each devoted to a "choir" of the nine:

| Faction | Worships | Doctrine & methods | Field signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **🌅 The Choir of the False Dawn** | Luxavor, Ashkarath | Zealot-priests of the counterfeit sunrise. Burn the unworthy *gently*, convert the rest. Masters of illusion-light and crowd-turning. | White-gold masks; ash that falls upward |
| **🌊 The Stillwater Communion** | Vormaela, Cryomara | Drowned-quiet infiltrators. They flood, freeze, and preserve — they call victims "the kept." Took Hyujon's harbor in one tide. | Frost-flowered doors; standing water that doesn't ripple |
| **🪨 The Rootless** | Bramblehex, Gorrundax | Saboteurs of soil and stone. Collapse the roads, rot the granaries, starve the cities into prayer. | Tunnels lined with wrong-growing moss |
| **👑 THE CROWNLESS PROCESSION** | **Nyxghul, Zerathuul, Voltrazar** | **The main Faction — the head of the snake.** Led by the **Veiled Cantor**, voice like a struck bell. They do not raid; they *prepare arrival*. Their project: widen the rift the Stormspire engine was answering, and walk the Hollow Crown out of Ghandra. | Erased records; witnesses who remember a hymn but not a face |

> **Design note:** the record-wiping of Aljay and Onnel carries the Crownless signature.
> The player should clock this in Chapter X — two chapters before anyone says it aloud.

### Chapter-by-chapter — THE CHRONICLE, ACT II

**Chapter VIII — The Silence of Legends** 📡
- 🗣 **Greggy**, his bluff on **Agdao Island** (he radios you the moment Ch. VII's report lands — the line crackles in a way Greggy's line never does: *worry*).
- 📍 Agdao. No dungeon — this is the act's overture. Greggy lays out everything: the dead
  radios, the wiped traces, the word *Anomalies*, the smoke still rising off **Hyujon**.
  He gives you the engine's "last word" from Ch. VII, decoded: not a countdown — **a
  response chant. Call sign: the Crownless.**
- 🎁 ◆1200, Greggy's **Relay Cipher** (relic) · ⏭ the cipher points at a fresh signal nest in the Thunderfen.

**Chapter IX — The Hollow Hymn** 🕯️
- 🏰 **The Ashen Chantry** — *new dungeon*, a burned chapel-warren beneath the Thunderfen
  ridge where the Mire's lightning never strikes (4 floors, Lv 24–30). The Anomalies'
  forward shrine: Choir of the False Dawn cells, captive wild Guardians "anointed" in ash.
- **Boss: the Ashen Cantor's warden — "Pyrelisk Unmade" (corrupted Pyrelisk, Lv 32)** —
  the first Anomaly-marked boss. It drops the **Hymnal Shard**: extract it.
- 🗣 Report to your **House master** (the Guild must hear it first — the Compact demands it).
  The masters convene all five Houses in one scene; the word *Anomalies* enters the record.
- 🎁 ◆3000, choice of stat gem, **Hymnal Shard** consumed into the journal · ⏭ the shard's verses name the *next* nest — and a courier from Agdao is already waiting.

**Chapter X — What the Storm Keeps** ⚡
- 📍 Back to **Greggy on Agdao**. He's read your guild report twice and the Hymnal verses
  nine times. There's a line in them about "the thief of the dawn" that makes him go quiet
  in a way thunder shouldn't. He won't say Aljay's name. He doesn't have to.
- He gives you the thing he climbed the bluff to guard: **the DAWNSHARD** — a splinter of
  Aurelia's first hearth, one of three. 🎁 **LEGENDARY ITEM: the Dawnshard** — used on any
  one Guardian, it **forcefully evolves it to its next stage, or surges it with levels, up
  to Lv 20** (whichever ceiling comes first). One use. Spend it like a legend would.
- 🎁 also ◆2000, Grand Elixir ×2 · ⏭ "Now go north, kid. Hyujon's still smoking, and
  somebody up there saw *why* it burned."

**Chapter XI — The Lantern of the North** 🏙️
- 🗺 **HYUJON** — *new map*: the high-tech northern city. Mag-rail spines, holo-lanterns,
  a harbor district still frost-flowered from the Stillwater tide, a midtown crater where
  the Crownless "prepared arrival" and failed — or *finished*, no one is sure.
- 🗣 **Marshal Ines Kovar** (city defense, blunt, missing an antenna-arm on her exo-rig),
  **Archivist Tem** (a records-keeper who *noticed the erasure happening live* and hid a
  backup in an air duct), and **a Stillwater defector** in the harbor cells.
- 🏰 **The Drowned Terminal** — *new dungeon*: Hyujon's flooded mag-rail underworks
  (4 floors, Lv 28–34). **Boss: Communion Tideling-Mother "Vormaela's Echo" (Lv 36).**
- The reveal, from Tem's duct backup: **eleven weeks ago a single unregistered traveler
  passed through Hyujon's north gate.** Alone. No Crawler, no companions, no luggage —
  just a lantern on his belt, unlit. He spoke to no one, registered nothing, and left no
  trail a historian, a marshal, or a cult could follow. The Anomalies hit the city *nine
  days later* — and the first thing they burned was the gate-records hall.
- **He went alone.** Not with Greggy. Not with Onnel. Whatever Aljay is doing, he cut
  every line behind him on purpose — which means the only thread left in the world that
  leads to him is the one person stubborn enough to re-derive his path from scratch:
  **Azrael.** Find the daughter, find the father.
- 🎁 ◆4500, Aether-grade Crawler schematic, **Tem's Backup** (relic) · ⏭ the cult is
  hunting *backward along his trail* — same as you. Race them to Azrael.

**Chapter XII — The Emberlark's Plea** 🔥
- 🗣 **AZRIN** — she finds *you*, the moment you step off the boat in Haven City.
  **Sixteen years old and already a legend's ledger**: a shelf of Intercontinental
  championship trophies and **one World Championship** — the youngest finals winner the
  Coliseum broadsheets can find a record of. Fights like sunrise. And right now she is
  *terrified* and hiding it badly. **Azrael is missing.** Her younger sister was
  **last seen at the University after her graduation** — she walked the stage, posted her
  Tamer license to the family hut herself, told Wren she had "one thing to check in the
  archives"… and never came out the shuttle side.
- 📍 University investigation (no dungeon — a detective chapter): 🗣 Archivist Wren (what
  Azrael requested: *the Legion-war sighting indexes — Veyl's own*), Historian Veyl (whose
  amber-trick Azrael copied — she pulled a trace of her **father**), Student Niko (the last
  to see her: "she was reading and then she just… *stood up*").
- Know who you're chasing: **Azrael is her father over again.** Stubborn past all
  argument, a quiet genius like her sister and her dad, and she loves Guardians the way
  Aljay does — as *friends*, never tools. Wren's note in the margin of the checkout slip:
  *"She thanked the library Guardian by name. Nobody knows the library Guardian's name."*
- And then Azrin's quiet, awful info-drop about their Dad, the thing she's been carrying
  all act: **before he vanished, Aljay came home one last time. He gathered his old
  Guardians — Firgara, Onthrofa, Vulfenix, all three, the full Ghandra lineup — and left
  before dawn without waking anyone, alone, telling no one. Not even Greggy. Not even
  Onnel.** He has not done that in fifteen years. You do not collect your war-team for a
  holiday — and you do not go *alone* unless you've decided nobody else is allowed to pay
  for it.
- 🎁 ◆2500, Azrin joins as a **story ally** (guest battles) · ⏭ Aljay left no trail at
  all. Azrael, re-deriving his path, left a faint one. Hers leads somewhere the Crownless
  are already singing.

**Chapter XIII — The Crownless Door** 👑 *(act finale — door left ajar)*
- 🏰 **The Unsung Vault** — *new dungeon*: a Crownless Procession sanctum beneath the old
  seal-shrine (5 floors, Lv 32–40). **Boss: the Veiled Cantor's herald — "Zerathuul's
  Shadow" (Lv 42)**, sung into shape from rift-residue.
- The extraction: the Cantor's hymnal ledger. It confirms everything in one cold line —
  *"The thief of the dawn has entered the door he built. **Alone.** The Crown stirs.
  Sing."* **Aljay went back into Ghandra — by himself, war-team and lantern, no Onnel, no
  Greggy, no goodbye.** The Anomalies are trying to follow him in — that's what Hyujon was
  for. And Azrael's re-derived trail doesn't end at the door. It *turns*: she found
  something her father missed, and went after it instead. The last line of her cipher-notes
  names a place no chart in Veyl's library carries: **Turmal.**
- 🎁 ◆8000, second **Dawnshard** · ⏭ **ACT III: THE TURMAL RUN.** Greggy stops pretending
  to be retired. Azrin stops asking permission. And an old friend of Aljay's has been
  waiting eleven weeks for someone to finally come asking the right questions.

## PART V — ACT III: **THE TURMAL RUN** *(the Anomalies Saga, Part Two — design bible)*

> *There is an island that never agreed to land. It hangs above its own shadow,
> holds a tournament every season, and asks no questions about the gold that funds it.
> Somebody should.*

### The stage — the Floating Island of TURMAL

**Turmal** 🗺 *(new region — the saga's second home)*: a floating island adrift above the
western sea, held aloft — the islanders shrug — "the way it always has been." Three faces
of it matter:

- **Turmal Above** — the terraced sky-city and the **Seasonal Tournament**: the loudest
  open circuit outside the Grand Coliseum. Brackets every season, broadcast crystals to
  four continents, and **Tournament Points** for every placement *(the only thing your
  Universal Rank eats — see the Coliseum's new ladder rules)*. The prize vaults are
  bottomless, and nobody on the island can tell you whose gold fills them. That's the job.
- **The Breathing Forest** — the island's deep interior canopy, where the trees inhale at
  dusk and exhale at dawn (the islanders set their clocks by it). Old, alive, and exactly
  the kind of place three young champions once chose to disappear into between seasons.
- **The Violet Garden** — *beneath* the island: a hanging under-grotto of violet
  bioluminescent bloom on the island's keel-stone, reached by winch-basket. Beautiful,
  unpatrolled, and — lately — *occupied*.

## PART VI — ACT IV: **THE RIP** *(the Anomalies Saga, Part Three — design bible)*

> *You wanted to see behind the curtain. Nobody warned you the curtain was a door,
> the door was three hundred years old, and the thing behind it was hungry.*

### Chapter-by-chapter — THE CHRONICLE, ACT IV

**Chapter XVIII — Behind the Curtain** 🏆
- 📍 **Turmal Above — the Seasonal finals.** You **win the Tournament** — the bracket, the
  broadcast, the lot (🎁 the saga's biggest single **Tournament Points** award, the
  champion's purse, and a Universal Rank moment the whole ladder was built for).
- Then the envelope: champions get invited behind the Sponsors' curtain. A private
  reception below the hospitality boxes. The wine is excellent. The hosts are charming.
  The freight elevator behind the cellar wall is **not on any blueprint** — and Louie's
  background checks gave you exactly one instruction for tonight: *get on it.*
- What you see at the bottom, from a catwalk, before the alarms find you: a cavernous
  **underground facility** in dark glass and old-empire alloy — and at its heart, held
  open in a lattice of humming machines, **a Rip in space and time**. A wound in the
  world's cloth, violet-black, *breathing*. And on the approach ramp below: robed
  Sponsors and Anomaly cantors together, leading caged, "anointed" **Guardians toward
  the light — sacrifices.** The cult and the money were never partners. **They were
  always the same thing.**
- ⏭ the alarms find you. There is exactly one way out, and it goes *down, through them*.

**Chapter XIX — The Null Cathedral** 🕳️
- 🏰 **THE NULL CATHEDRAL** — *new dungeon, unlike any before it*: the Sponsors' facility
  proper. **Dark-tech theme** — black glass, dead-light conduits, server-reliquaries,
  ritual circles drawn in circuitry, the Rip's violet glow bleeding through grates in
  every floor. **No wild Guardians. None.** Nothing lives down here that wasn't brought
  down here. The dungeon is silence, machinery, hymn — and **a boss-level Anomaly waiting
  on every floor**:
  | Floor | Boss | Notes |
  |---|---|---|
  | B1 | **Choirmaster Velhand** (False Dawn, Lv 46) | the reception's charming host, mask finally off |
  | B2 | **Mother Neris of the Stillwater** (Lv 47) | the Violet Garden's "preservation" was her supply line |
  | B3 | **The Ossuary Knight** (Rootless, Lv 48) | grown into its own armor; the facility's oldest servant |
  | B4 | **Cantor-Second of the Procession** (Lv 50) | the Veiled Cantor's right hand — and the floor's altar is *active* |
  *(three boss battles minimum on any path; all four on a full clear)*
- The B4 victory interrupts the rite mid-hymn — the caged Guardians are freed (a wave of
  them join the bestiary as rescue-bonded encounters), the lattice destabilizes, and the
  Rip narrows to a seam. Not closed. **Narrowed.**
- 🎁 ◆10000, Mystic-grade reagents, the **Reliquary Ledger** · ⏭ and the Ledger is the
  real treasure — because it goes back further than anyone sane would have guessed.

**Chapter XX — Three Hundred Years** 📜
- 📍 Reading room: Louie's loft, with Greggy on the relay and a Duneward Assembly
  archivist confirming dates. The Reliquary Ledger's entries run back **three hundred
  years — unbroken.** The Sponsors' demonic rite — Guardians fed to the Rip, season after
  season — predates the Seasonal Tournament, predates the Grand Houses, predates the
  Legion War itself. Cross one date with Part I's timeline and go cold: *~300–212 years
  ago, the Old Empire "drowned in its own ambition."* It didn't drown. **It was bled** —
  the first Sponsors wore imperial signets, and the empire's engine-craft was built to
  hold this wound open.
- The implication nobody says aloud for a full minute: the Anomalies didn't rise to
  worship the nine corrupted generals. **The cult is older than the corruption.** Nine
  Guardians went wrong fifteen years ago — and the thing they went wrong *near* has been
  fed for three centuries.
- And one more entry, recent, personal: a budget line for **"reputation management,"**
  dated nine years back, against a name the tournament world used to chant: **IVAN
  LAWRENCE.** ⏭ the cult doesn't only erase records. It erases *people* — and one of them
  is still alive.

**Chapter XXI — The Man They Broke First** 🥀
- The dossier, assembled with Louie and the Ledger: **Ivan Lawrence** — **childhood
  friend of Aljay**, grew up three doors apart, first rivals and then brothers in
  everything but blood. **Four-time Intercontinental Champion** in his own right — never
  the World crown, never cared; "someone has to lose finals to Aljay with style." Nine
  years ago he got close to a Sponsor's books — too close — and the Anomalies did to him
  what they do: **sabotaged matches in his name, planted evidence, broke his image** on
  every broadcast crystal on four continents. He wasn't beaten. He was *deleted* — the
  first live demonstration of the machine that later wiped Aljay's trail.
- He spent the years since **hiding with his family** where broadcast crystals don't
  reach: **NEW SALMONAN** 🗺 — *new map*: a lively rural town of terraced paddies, river
  piers, market mornings and loud kitchens, where everybody knows everybody and nobody
  asks where the quiet champion on the hill came from. New Salmonan **loves the Big
  Three** — Aljay, Greggy and Onnel saved this valley *countless* times in the war years
  (flood, siege, and once, famously, a wedding), and the town keeps a mural of them by
  the river gate, repainted every spring.
- 📍 The chapter: find Ivan (he won't be found until the town decides you're safe — win
  the market folk over first), then **clear his name**: the Reliquary Ledger plus the
  Cantor-Second's seized hymnal is proof no broadsheet can ignore. The broadcast goes out
  from the town's one battered relay tower, with the whole valley crowded around it.
- The payoff scenes: Ivan's family on the porch hearing his real record read back out
  loud; the town's **gratitude festival** for the player — New Salmonan does not do small
  thank-yous; and Ivan, finally able to stand in daylight, telling you the thing only a
  childhood friend would know: **where Aljay goes when he wants to be unfindable — and
  what he was like the last time he had *that look*.** The saga's next heading, given
  over river fish and too much rice wine.
- 🎁 ◆8000, **Ivan's Champion Band** (relic — a 4-time champion's gauntlet, +TP% from
  future placements), New Salmonan as a rest-town (free Sanctum-grade inn, market), and a

  and a seam in the world that is narrowed, not closed.

## PART VII — ACT V: **THE FORETALES** *(the Anomalies Saga, Part Four — design bible)*

> *You cleared one man's name and the world's news network corrected you within
> thirty-six hours. Question: who corrects the network?*

### The reveal that reframes the whole saga

The Sponsors backed by the Anomalies were a **finger**. The hand is **FORETALES** — the
broadcast-and-broadsheet network on every crystal, every pier, every plaza on four
continents. The news everyone grew up inside. And it has been writing the news **before
it happens**: countless events covered — and covered *up* — for scheme and profit.
Sabotaged matches. Vanished candidates. Buried disasters. Manufactured darlings. Ivan
Lawrence wasn't their first deletion; he was a rehearsal they finally performed in
public.

**The sixteen-year interregnum.** It has been sixteen years since any continent seated a
proper leader, and the arithmetic is always the same: the front-runner withdraws, or
drowns in scandal, or simply *stops being mentioned* — and the polls (every one of them
run through a Foretales subsidiary) discover the public "never really wanted them
anyway." Hidden funding underneath, manipulated polls on top. **It runs deep. It runs
wide. It runs violent.**

**The glaze.** And yet the Big Three shine from every crystal — anniversary specials,
soft-lit documentaries, endless retrospectives. It is an act. A standing directive,
spooled at the heart of the Mirrorhouse, names the only three people on four continents
Foretales is forbidden to touch:

> *"Standing directive, all desks, regarding the Dawnflame, the Stormheart and the
> Worldroot: GLAZE. DO NOT TOUCH. **NOT YET. SOON.**"*

That is why Aljay — why any of the Big Three — was never close to the media. You can
count the true interviews and real documentaries on one hand; everything else is
fabricated… *and something more.* The glaze is a leash that hasn't been pulled yet.
Aljay counted the same hand everyone else could have, and drew the conclusion nobody
else dared.
